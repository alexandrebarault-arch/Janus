## 6 — Why the time-dependent BD reduction matters

After eliminating `(Phi,B,varphi,b)` on Candidate C, use original coordinates

`q=(Sigma,S,u_g,u_f)`.

The reduced kinetic Hessian has rank 3. Its null vector satisfies

`u_f / Sigma = 1/(n H_f)`

at fixed physical variables, matching De Felice's scalar variable

`Y2 = delta_phi_f + sqrt(X_f)/H_f * (Delta S/6 - Sigma)`.

With `u_f=delta_phi_f/phi_dot_f`, a convenient normalized physical variable is

`Y2hat = u_f - (Sigma + k^2 S/6)/(n H_f)`.

Therefore

`u_f = Y2hat + R (Sigma + k^2 S/6)`, `R=1/(n H_f)`,

and its velocity contains

`u_f_dot = Y2hat_dot + R(Sigma_dot+k^2 S_dot/6) + Rdot(Sigma+k^2 S/6)`.

Dropping `Rdot` reproduces the old false saturation. Keeping the time dependence, integrating the null coordinate by parts, and eliminating it algebraically restores the pure-gravity high-k limit exactly:

`lim_{k->infinity} omega^2 / (k^2/a^2) = 1`.
