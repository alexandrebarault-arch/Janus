# JANUS research verdict — V1

Date: 2026-10-11

## Question

If we ignore reputation and consensus and instead audit the equations, repair what can be repaired, and continue the underlying physical idea with modern mathematical tools, does Jean-Pierre Petit's Janus program lead to a convincing cosmological theory?

## Short answer

**Not in its published exact form.**

But one part of the intuition survives serious scrutiny:

> Two positive-energy matter sectors coupled to distinct interacting metrics can exhibit an emergent cross-sector repulsive force and relative-sector dynamics without introducing fundamental negative kinetic energy.

That surviving statement is narrower than Petit's Janus model and has different observational signatures.

## What did not survive

### 1. Published Janus as a closed fundamental theory

`FAIL / INCOMPLETE`

The general nonlinear interaction is not specified sufficiently to derive a unique complete relativistic theory, and the 2015 constrained variation does not independently derive the two advertised field equations.

### 2. Exact universal `+G / -G` force law in the healthy two-metric continuation

`FAIL`

The modern ghost-free linear spin-2 realization does not reproduce an equal-strength `-G` cross force. In the canonical short-distance linear limit, the normalized cross slow-matter response approaches `-G/3`.

### 3. Fundamental negative energy as a necessary mechanism

`NOT REQUIRED`

Effective repulsion appears without assigning a fundamentally negative Hamiltonian to the twin matter sector. The negative-energy postulate is therefore not justified by necessity in the continuation program.

### 4. Negative gravitational lensing by an isolated twin-sector positive-energy mass

`FAIL IN JANUS-R LINEAR REGIME`

The complete null-ray calculation gives convergent cross lensing that tends to zero in the regime where the slow-matter cross force becomes repulsive. Matter and photons respond differently because the helicity-0 trace coupling affects nonrelativistic matter but not a traceless photon stress tensor.

### 5. Simple late linear growth as the origin of dramatic cosmic segregation

`TOO WEAK`

The maximally unscreened linear relative mode grows only as approximately `a^1.186` in an Einstein-de Sitter diagnostic. This is faster than the ordinary common mode but not enough to create extreme late segregation from an initially negligible relative perturbation.

### 6. Simply adding more healthy metric sectors to recover exact `-G`

`FAIL AS A GENERIC LINEAR FIX`

Within the standard canonical massless+massive spin-2 decomposition, mode completeness preserves the characteristic `-1/3` slow-matter cross asymptote and zero photon cross response at high momentum.

## What survived

### 1. Cross-sector repulsion can be physically real

`PASS`

Ghost-free bimetric gravity with two separately coupled positive-energy matter sectors can produce a genuine repulsive acceleration between sectors in a defined regime.

### 2. Nonlinear screening can restore GR-like behavior

`PASS IN PRINCIPLE`

Published nonlinear analyses show that the helicity-0 mode can enter a cosmological Vainshtein regime and remove linear early-Universe instabilities over appropriate parameter regions.

### 3. The two-matter relative mode is a genuine nonlinear degree of freedom

`PASS`

The explicit nonlinear scalar equation contains the weighted difference

`rho_g delta_g - rho_f delta_f`.

A visible void accompanied by a twin overdensity strongly excites this relative mode. This is mathematically close to the segregation idea motivating Janus.

### 4. Self-acceleration without an explicit cosmological constant is possible in the underlying bimetric skeleton

`PASS FOR STANDARD BIMETRIC BACKGROUNDS`

Healthy finite branches exist for which the spin-2 interaction drives late acceleration. This does not yet prove the two-matter JANUS-R cosmology.

### 5. There is a falsifiable distinctive signature

`PASS`

The cleanest surviving signature is **not negative lensing**. It is a scale-dependent mismatch between:

- slow-matter/velocity response;
- photon/Weyl-lensing response.

For the equal anti-correlated linear mode,

`A_lens/A_dyn = 3/4`.

At the special crossover `x=1/2`, lensing can look exactly GR-like while dynamics is enhanced by `4/3`.

## Current observations

Current data do not provide positive evidence for this effect.

- 2025 DESI-Legacy/Planck void lensing agrees closely with calibrated Lambda-CDM mocks (`A_kappa = 1.016 +/- 0.054`).
- 2025 DESI/BOSS + weak-lensing `E_G` measurements report consistency with Planck GR+Lambda-CDM to redshift approximately one.
- 2026 DESI full-shape clustering finds no evidence for the tested scale-dependent modified-gravity model; although its scalar `f(R)` numerical bound cannot be transferred to spin-2 JANUS-R, it demonstrates high sensitivity to new gravitational scales.

Therefore a dominant, order-unity late Janus-like relative mode is already **disfavored as a generic explanation of large-scale structure**. A smaller, screened or crossover-tuned component remains open until the exact two-matter transfer functions are fitted.

## Final V1 verdict

The strongest defensible conclusion is:

> **Jean-Pierre Petit appears to have identified a physically interesting target — gravitational segregation between two sectors — but the exact mechanism he proposes is not currently the one that survives the strongest mathematical checks. A healthier continuation replaces fundamental negative mass by two positive-energy sectors and spin-2 mixing. This continuation can produce repulsion, but not the exact universal Janus force law and not Petit's negative-lensing signature. Current observations leave room only for a constrained version whose decisive test is a joint lensing-plus-dynamics calculation.**
